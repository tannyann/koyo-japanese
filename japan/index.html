The small dish you didn't order

理
The reasonCalled otōshi, it is widely treated as a seat charge with a welcome bite, not a mistake.

Why the train is so quiet

見
You noticeA packed carriage, yet almost no one is talking — and phones are on silent.
理
The reasonShared space is seen as everyone's space; not adding noise is a quiet form of consideration.
動
Your moveKeep voices low, skip calls, and wear your backpack on your front.

The alleys that rose from the ruins

見
You noticeNarrow lanes of tiny bars, right beside giant department stores.
理
The reasonLanes like Omoide Yokocho are said to trace back to postwar open-air markets near the station.
動
Your moveMany bars seat only a few — peek in, smile, and ask “Daijōbu desu ka?”

弐 · Manners

Six small things that make the trip smoother.

作法帳

一01 / 06

Shoes off where floors rise

A raised step at the entrance usually means shoes come off. Look for slippers — and never wear them onto tatami.

二02 / 06

No tip, just thanks

Tipping is not part of the culture and can cause confusion. A clear “Gochisōsama deshita” after a meal goes further.

三03 / 06

Stand left in Tokyo

On escalators, Tokyo locals generally keep left (Osaka is often the opposite). Follow the line in front of you.

四04 / 06

Chopsticks rest, never stand

Chopsticks upright in rice recall funeral rites. Lay them across the holder or the edge of your bowl.

五05 / 06

Take your rubbish with you

Public bins are rare. Most people carry rubbish home — a small bag in your day pack helps.

六06 / 06

A slight bow says a lot

A small nod when you thank, greet or apologise is perfectly polite. No need for a deep bow.

The hardest step
is the curtain.

A cloth curtain hung in a doorway usually means the place is open. Ducking through it is how you walk in — and the walk ends with you doing exactly that.

案内人 · The guide

“I grew up taking these customs for granted. Travelling abroad taught me how strange — and how beautiful — they look from the outside.”

I'm Koyo, born and raised in Japan. After backpacking and living overseas, I started noticing the questions visitors kept asking: Why is it so quiet? Why the small dish? Am I allowed in here?

This is where I answer them — through short stories, walks around Tokyo, and Japanese lessons. I'm also learning five languages myself, so I know how it feels to be the one trying to understand.

伍 · Good to know

Questions, answered.

Do I need to speak Japanese for the tour?+

Not at all. Tours are in English, and you'll pick up a few useful phrases along the way.

What does the tour fee include?+

Guiding only. Food, drinks and any transport are paid by guests directly, so you only pay for what you choose.

Is the night walk okay if I don't drink?+

Yes. Many places serve soft drinks and food, and the walk is about how the area works and how to feel at home in it.

How do I book or pay?+

Through the booking page. If it isn't open yet, send a message on Instagram and you'll be the first to hear.
