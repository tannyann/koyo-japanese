箸 Izakaya · Shinjuku

The small dish you didn't order

Scene
You sit down and a tiny appetizer arrives — and later a “table charge” on the bill.
Reason
Called otōshi, it is widely treated as a seat charge with a welcome bite, rather than a mistake.
Your move
Enjoy it. If the budget matters, ask about the charge before you sit.
静 Trains · Everyday

Why the train is so quiet

Scene
A packed carriage, yet almost no one is talking — and phones are on silent.
Reason
Shared spaces are seen as everyone's space; not adding noise is a quiet form of consideration.
Your move
Keep voices low, skip phone calls, and wear your backpack on your front.
灯 History · Shinjuku

The alleys that rose from the ruins

Scene
Narrow lanes packed with tiny bars, right next to giant department stores.
Reason
Areas like Omoide Yokocho are said to trace back to postwar open-air markets near the station.
Your move
Many bars seat only a few people — peek in, smile, and ask “Daijōbu desu ka?”

Manners, without the stress

Six things that make your trip smoother.

Nobody expects visitors to be perfect. These small habits simply make every encounter warmer.

一

Shoes off where floors rise

A raised step at the entrance usually means shoes come off. Look for slippers — and never wear them onto tatami.

二

No tip, just thanks

Tipping is not part of the culture and can cause confusion. A clear “Gochisōsama deshita” after a meal goes further.

三

Stand left in Tokyo

On escalators, Tokyo locals generally keep to the left (Osaka is often the opposite). Follow the line in front of you.

四

Chopsticks rest, never stand

Sticking chopsticks upright in rice recalls funeral rites. Lay them across the holder or the edge of your bowl.

五

Take your rubbish with you

Public bins are rare. Most people carry their rubbish home — a small bag in your day pack helps.

六

A slight bow says a lot

A small nod of the head when you thank, greet or apologise is perfectly polite. No need for a deep bow.

About

“I grew up taking these customs for granted. Travelling abroad taught me how strange — and how beautiful — they look from the outside.”

I'm Koyo, born and raised in Japan. After backpacking and living overseas, I started noticing the questions visitors kept asking: Why is it so quiet? Why the small dish? Am I allowed in here?

This is where I answer them — through short stories, walks around Tokyo, and Japanese lessons. I'm also learning five languages myself, so I know how it feels to be the one trying to understand.

Questions

Good to know

Do I need to speak Japanese for the tour?

Not at all. Tours are in English, and you'll pick up a few useful phrases along the way.

What does the tour fee include?

The fee covers guiding. Food, drinks and any transport are paid by guests directly, so you only pay for what you choose.

Is the night walk suitable if I don't drink alcohol?

Yes. Many places serve soft drinks and food, and the focus of the walk is on how the area works and how to feel at home in it.

How do I book or pay?

Booking and payment are handled through the booking page linked above. If it isn't open yet, send a message on Instagram and you'll be the first to hear.
